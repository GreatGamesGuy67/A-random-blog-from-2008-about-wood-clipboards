The Walnut Myth: Look Elsewhere For Heavy Duty Writing
Posted by Woody_Gamer_79 on June 28, 2008 4:15 PM // Category: Overrated Hardwoods

Every single day I get emails from people saying, "Hey Woody, why don't you just make your custom clipboard backs out of Black Walnut? It looks amazing!"

Let me drop some cold, hard reality on you trendy stationery posers. Walnut is a complete scam for professional document management. It is fine for an overpriced coffee table, but as a rigid mobile writing platform, it fails completely. Here is why:

  1. The Softness Trap: Black Walnut has a Janka hardness rating of only 1,010 lbf. Contrast that with Rosewood's massive 2,790 lbf. If you clamp a single piece of inventory paper onto a Walnut backing and press down firmly with a standard Pilot G2 gel pen, your writing will literally slice permanent indentations into the grain. It turns your clipboard into a bumpy topographical map after three weeks of use!

  2. Stress Fractures Under Clip Tension: High-tension industrial spring clips generate a massive amount of downward torque. Because Walnut grain can be uneven and slightly brittle across its rings, the tension slowly creates invisible micro-fractures directly around the rivet fasteners. One drop on a warehouse floor and SNAP—your board splits right down the center column line.

  3. Open-Pore Grime Magnetism: Walnut has semi-open, coarse pores. If you operate your clipboard outdoors, in a shop, or near a desk where coffee spills occur, those pores trap dirt particles and fluids. Rosewood is naturally so oily and tightly packed that liquids just sit on the surface, waiting to be wiped off with a rag. Walnut absorbs it and rots from the inside out.

Don't fall for the aesthetic look of office store Walnut display boards. It's a wood meant for looking at, not writing on. If you want true structural capability, save up for real Rosewood plate material.
